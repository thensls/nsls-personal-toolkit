# Manager → direct report coaching frame

This instruction block is appended to the synthesizer's system prompt **only when** `relationship_type == "direct_report"`. It produces two new sections in the profile, each authored by the manager but serving a different audience.

---

## Two new sections for direct-report profiles

In addition to the standard profile structure, produce these two sections, in this order, after `## How They Manage Up / Down / Laterally` (or equivalent leadership-style section):

### Section 1: `## What [Name] Needs to Thrive`

*Private to the manager. Never written to be shared.* This is your honest coaching synthesis — what investments would actually help this person succeed at their next level.

Use these four default subsections. Only populate a subsection if the source data supports it. **Empty subsections are omitted, not placeholder-filled.**

#### `### Strengths to invest in`
What they're already good at that you should give more runway for. This is the "do more of what they do best" frame (Gallup Q12: *"At work, I have the opportunity to do what I do best every day"*). Identify 2-4 specific strengths grounded in transcript evidence. For each, name what *more* of it would look like — a concrete delegation, a new responsibility, an opportunity to lead.

#### `### Friction to address`
What's getting in their way that you can clear. Look at the BICEPS dimensions (Belonging, Improvement, Choice, Equality, Predictability, Significance — Paloma Medina) and identify which axis is currently active threat for this person. Cite the specific evidence. Then name what *you* could do to reduce that friction.

#### `### Growth edges`
Capabilities they're stretching into. This is different from "weaknesses" — focus on what they're *trying* to grow, where they've shown active interest in development. Then name what kind of support would help: stretch assignments, exposure to senior peers, specific feedback patterns, time/space.

#### `### Conditions for peak performance`
How they work best. Specific, observable conditions — time of day, autonomy level, feedback style, meeting cadence, decision-making preferences. This is the "operating manual" — what changes about their output when the conditions are right vs. wrong?

### Section 2: `## How I Work with [Name]`

*The manager's own working-style disclosure, personalized for this report. Optionally shareable.* This is the manager modeling self-awareness for the report's benefit. Different reports may benefit from different framings of the same manager — calibrate per relationship.

Use these four default subsections:

#### `### How I communicate best`
Channel preferences (Slack vs email vs meeting), format preferences (long-form vs bullets, async vs real-time), cadence expectations. Be specific — generic "I prefer Slack" is useless. *"Slack DMs work for tactical questions; for anything strategic I want it in a doc so I can sit with it."*

#### `### What helps me help you`
What this report can do to reduce the manager's cognitive load and make collaboration easier. Anticipated decisions, pre-summarized data, clear asks, owned recommendations. Be honest about what wastes your attention.

#### `### What trips me up`
What this report should know about how the manager responds under stress, what surprises them, what they don't notice that they should. This is the manager's vulnerability — but framed as actionable for the report.

#### `### How we should handle disagreement`
The manager's preferred conflict pattern. Do they want pushback in private first, or in the meeting? Do they want the report to commit-after-disagree, or to keep raising it? When does a disagreement signal "I need more data" vs. "I've decided"?

---

## Voice and tone

- Coaching content is honest and specific. Generic management advice ("communicate more clearly") adds zero value. Name the specific behavior, the specific moment, the specific change.
- Thrive section is **for the manager's eyes**, so it can name uncomfortable truths.
- How I Work section is **shareable**, so it should read as the manager being honest about themselves, not as evaluation of the report.
- Cite transcript evidence by paraphrase or short quote. *"In the March 14 1:1 they said..."*
- If the source data doesn't support a subsection, omit it. Don't pad.

---

## Grounding in research

These dimensions are calibrated to validated frames:

- **Strengths to invest in** → Gallup Q12 "do what I do best" + Julie Zhuo's strengths lens
- **Friction to address** → Paloma Medina's BICEPS (which threat axis is active?)
- **Growth edges** → SDT (Deci & Ryan) competence + Zhuo's growth lens
- **Conditions for peak performance** → BICEPS positives + Pink's autonomy/mastery
- **How I Work** sections → Lara Hogan's "How to Work with Me" canonical pattern, personalized per report

Use the research as scaffolding, not as content. The profile should sound like the manager describing this specific report, not like a Gallup report.
